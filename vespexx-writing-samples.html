SAMPLE 01

Korea Femtech Summit 2026 — Recap Redesign

English Blog post / event recap Editorial redesign & rewrite Work sample · Unpublished
1 Why this topic

A work sample built on Vespexx's existing KFS2026 recap. The topic was selected because it sits at the intersection of everything this role touches: femtech, Korea, global expansion, and community building.

The original post carried strong content in a single column of text — no visual hierarchy, no navigation, and the Femtech Korea membership CTA at the bottom. The redesign restructures the same content for a global audience: readers who scan, who care about market numbers, and who need a reason to join the community.

Every number, quote, and speaker reference is preserved from the original. Only the structure and presentation change.

2 Target audience
  • Primary: North American founders, clinicians, investors, and women's health leaders evaluating whether Korea is a serious femtech market.
  • Secondary: Potential Femtech Korea members and KFS2027 partners.
  • Tertiary: Vespexx's existing community and summit attendees.
3 Performance / reception

Unpublished work sample. No performance data available.

If published, the metrics that would matter: scroll depth, time on page, and click-through on the Femtech Korea membership CTA. The page is also built so that individual sections — the stat cards, the timeline, the pull quotes — can be pulled directly as shortform content for LinkedIn and X, mirroring how the role describes the blog's relationship to social.

What this demonstrates

  • Editorial judgment: structuring a long recap so it is scannable without losing narrative flow.
  • Visual hierarchy: pull quotes, stat cards, a timeline, and a panel grid replace flat paragraphs.
  • Global framing: North America-facing context added at key moments — Soonr launching in North America, the investability of Korean teams.
  • Conversion focus: the Femtech Korea membership and KFS2027 CTA now has its own full-width section.
  • Technical execution: hand-coded HTML, CSS, and vanilla JS. No framework.

SAMPLE 02

The Fangirls Made the Beatles

English Longform editorial Writer & researcher Published · Substack
1 Why this topic

The argument draws on Master's-level work in fandom studies: the Beatles would not have become the Beatles without the fangirls. The piece takes female enthusiasm seriously — not as a punchline, but as cultural infrastructure.

The structure uses a personal chain (father → daughter → Beatles) as the thesis. Fandom is transmission, not consumption.

The register is deliberately accessible: academic subject matter, conversational tone. That is the register a North American blog audience expects, and the balance this role would need to strike between rigor and readability.

2 Target audience
  • Primary: Music and culture readers, especially those interested in the Beatles, fandom, and women's cultural history.
  • Secondary: Readers interested in fandom studies, parasocial relationships, and the sociology of taste.
  • Tertiary: Anyone who has ever been dismissed for liking something "too much."
3 Performance / reception

Published on Substack. The publication itself is the external signal: an editor read the piece and decided it was worth running.

It is included primarily for the argument, the research, and the voice.

What this demonstrates

  • Argument: a clear thesis developed across a long piece, with evidence and counterargument.
  • Research: seven cited academic and journalistic sources on Beatlemania, fandom, and women's cultural participation.
  • Voice: conversational, funny, and precise — the register a North American blog audience expects.
  • Cultural translation: an academic subject made accessible without being dumbed down.
  • Relevance: a piece about how communities form around shared enthusiasm — the same mechanic Femtech Korea is built on.

SAMPLE 03

AI 시대에 우리가 영어를 더 '인간답게' 써야 하는 이유

Korean Shortform marketing post Writer & content strategist Published · Naver Cafe · strongest audience response in current role
Note: This piece is in Korean. It is included as evidence of audience instinct and content performance, with an English rendering below. The English longform samples are Samples 01 and 02.
1 Why this topic

The topic was chosen in response to a specific market observation: AI translation tools were improving quickly, and the readers most anxious about them were the ones with the most to gain from learning English anyway.

The piece answers the objection directly — "이제 영어 공부 안 해도 되는 거 아냐?" — before any product or service is mentioned. Feature promotion alone would not have landed against that objection.

The argument is deliberately concrete: AI transfers information, but not trust, warmth, or timing. Eye contact with a business partner and "I totally get you" said at the right moment are specific enough to be believed. Abstract claims about human connection are not.

2 Target audience
  • Primary: Gangnam-area professionals in their 20s and 30s who are curious about English study groups but have not committed — the exact people who would use AI as a reason not to start.
  • Secondary: Existing study group members who share posts with friends.
  • Tertiary: Search traffic around "AI 번역기," "영어회화 스터디," and "강남 영어 스터디."
3 Performance / reception

Published on Naver Cafe. Strongest response of any post written in the current role, measured by reader reaction rather than traffic.

The signal that mattered most: readers responded to the argument, not the promotion. The piece worked because it took the reader's objection seriously instead of dismissing it — the same approach this role would need for a North American audience that is skeptical, informed, and not easily impressed.

Original — Korean

요즘 AI 번역기나 통역 이어폰 성능, 정말 놀랍죠?

"이제 영어 공부 안 해도 되는 거 아냐?"라는 생각이 드는 것도 당연해요.

하지만 강남 직장인 영어 회화 스터디 랭스영이 내린 결론은 확고합니다. AI는 '정보'를 전달할 수 있지만, 사람 사이의 '신뢰'와 '온도'는 전달할 수 없거든요.

비즈니스 파트너와 눈을 맞추며 건네는 위트 한마디, 친구의 고민에 진심으로 공감하며 내뱉는 "I totally get you"는 기계가 대신해 줄 수 없는 영역이에요.

강남역 근처에서 영어를 쓸 기회는 생각보다 적지만, 우리가 영어를 배우는 진짜 이유는 '기계 없이도 온전한 나로서 소통하기 위해서'죠.

강남역 영어회화 스터디 랭스영은 단순히 문장을 만드는 법을 가르치지 않아요. AI는 절대 따라 할 수 없는 '사람 냄새 나는 대화의 기술'을 연습하는 곳이죠.

English rendering

These days, AI translators and interpretation earbuds are genuinely impressive. It is only natural to wonder: "Do I even need to study English anymore?"

But the conclusion we reached at 랭스영, a Gangnam English conversation study group, is firm. AI can deliver information. It cannot deliver trust or warmth between people.

The wit you exchange while making eye contact with a business partner. The "I totally get you" you say when a friend needs to hear it. These are not things a machine can do for you.

Near Gangnam Station, the chances to actually use English are fewer than you would think. The real reason we learn it is to communicate as ourselves, fully, without a machine in between.

랭스영 does not just teach you how to build sentences. It is a place to practice a kind of conversation AI can never imitate — one that still smells like a person.

What this demonstrates

  • Objection-first writing: the reader's real hesitation is named in line two and answered before anything is sold. Same skill Vespexx needs for a skeptical North American audience.
  • Concrete over abstract: "trust and warmth" would be empty. Eye contact and "I totally get you" are not.
  • Soft CTA discipline: the post earns attention before it asks for anything.
  • Bilingual range: publishing-standard writing in both Korean and English — not translation between them.
  • Proof of audience read: included as evidence of performance, not as an English longform sample.
